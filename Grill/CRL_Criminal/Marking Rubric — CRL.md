# Marking Rubric — CRL

> **What this is.** The criminal translation of [[IRAC Rubrics]] (Legal Analysis 40 · Organisation 25 · Writing 30) and [[The ELEMENTS of STYLE]]. The generic rubric supplies the bands; this file supplies **what the bands mean in criminal law** — charge precision, element architecture, case mastery, the deductive/inductive split, and defence sequencing.

**Honesty note.** There is no corpus of marked criminal A-scripts in the vault (unlike the contract seniors' scripts). The bands below are therefore **provisional**: they are the generic rubric applied to the criminal summaries and past papers, not an empirical sample. Say so when marking.

---

## §1 The weights

| Strand | Weight | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal Analysis** | 40% | Issue · Rule & explanation · Synthesis · Application · Counter-arguments |
| **Organisation** | 25% | IRAC · roadmap · headers · signposting · structure |
| **Writing** | 30% | Language · tone · citation · proofreading |

The remaining 5% is not stated in the rubric; do not invent it. Mark out of the three strands and say the bands are indicative.

## §2 The CRL translation

### 2.1 Legal Analysis (40%)

| Criterion | In criminal law, this means | Below Average | Average | Above Average |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Issue identification** | The **charge map**: which offence, which subsection, which limb; which defence; which counterfactual delta. Priority: gateways before factors. | Misses the offence or defence; frames the issue at the wrong level ("is he guilty?" instead of "which element fails?"); ignores the counterfactual | Identifies the main offence and one or two defences; some sub-issues underdeveloped | Identifies every material offence/defence/sub-issue, orders them by gateway, and isolates the counterfactual's single element |
| **Rule & rule explanation** | **Statute first**, then the case's **unique contribution** and **pinpoint**. The rule is the specific test, not the offence label. | Recites elements with no authority; wrong section; case cited for the wrong proposition; no pinpoint | Some authority, reasonable account, but gaps | Precise section + case + pinpoint + *why* the case contributes that proposition; distinguishes obiter |
| **Synthesis** | Cases in conversation: the line of authority, the tension, the boundary. Not a case list. | Case-by-case description; no proposition | Some synthesis, still fragmented | Builds a coherent proposition from the cases; explains the doctrinal and policy logic; places the present facts on the line |
| **Application** | Facts → element, in the **right mode** (D or I); material facts, not conclusions; analogy/distinction; counter-arguments; a landing. | Conclusory; no facts; "the accused satisfied the elements"; leaps from rule to conclusion | Competent but uneven; some analogies; some uncertainty acknowledged | Thorough; uses the *material* facts; floor/ceiling for degree standards; genuine analogy/distinction; confronts the defence's best case; lands |
| **Counter-arguments / adverse authority** | The defence's best argument; the case that cuts the other way; the unresolved point. | Ignores or dismisses | Brief, formulaic | Fairly engages, then distinguishes/limits/outweighs; explains why the landing survives |

### 2.2 Organisation (25%)

| Criterion | In criminal law, this means | Above Average looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **IRAC** | Each issue gets Issue → Rule → Application → Conclusion; no global rule-dump at the start | The reader can see the element being proved, fact by fact |
| **Roadmap** | At the start: the charges, the order, the defences, the counterfactual | "I will first consider X; if X fails, Y does not arise" |
| **Headers** | Accused-by-accused, offence-by-offence, element-by-element | "A. Theft — (i) movement — (ii) property — …" |
| **Signposting** | "Having established the actus reus, I turn to fault"; "That conclusion makes it unnecessary to consider…" | Smooth transitions; no re-litigation |
| **Structure of a fake-judgment appeal** | Ground 1, Ground 2, Ground 3 — each: the judge's proposition → the error → the correct law → the consequence | Not a fresh IRAC essay |

### 2.3 Writing (30%)

| Criterion | In criminal law, this means | Above Average looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Language** | Short, active, precise; no "the accused would have been said to have…" | "D intended to cause death: he aimed at the neck." |
| **Tone** | Objective, measured; no moralising, no advocacy beyond the law | "The better view is… because…" |
| **Citation** | Section + case + pinpoint; the pinpoint must exist | "s 300(c); *Lim Poh Lye* [20]" — not "the case of *Lim*" |
| **Proofreading** | Names, sections, pinpoints, arithmetic | No "s 304A(b)" when the charge is s 304A(a) |

## §3 The twelve fatal errors

| # | Error | What it looks like | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Wrong offence/subsection** | Charging s 406 when the facts support s 408; s 304A(a) when the statute bifurcates | Charge map first; check the aggravated form and the current numbering |
| 2 | **Reciting elements without applying** | "The elements of theft are… The accused committed theft." | After each element: the fact that proves it and the fact that cuts against it |
| 3 | **Lumping physical and fault elements** | "The accused dishonestly moved the property" as one element | Separate actus reus and mens rea; state the fault for each physical element |
| 4 | **Confusing intention/knowledge/rashness/negligence** | "He was negligent, so he intended…" | Use the fault spectrum; the offence's fault element decides the vocabulary |
| 5 | **Case cited for the wrong proposition** | *Hue An Li* for negligence; *Tan Chor Jin* for consent; *Er Joo* for conspiracy when the point is dishonesty | The Case Mastery Ledger's "common misuse" column |
| 6 | **No pinpoint** | "As held in *Wee Teong Boo*…" | Pinpoint every proposition; if you cannot, the proposition is not verified |
| 7 | **Name-dropping without material facts** | "This is like *Lam Leng Hung*." | State the material facts that make it like *Lam* — or it is not like *Lam* |
| 8 | **Defences before elements** | Running private defence before proving the offence | Element first; defence second; negating defences are part of fault |
| 9 | **Ignoring burden/standard** | "The accused must prove private defence beyond reasonable doubt"; "the prosecution must disprove s 79 beyond reasonable doubt" | Check each defence: s 79 and s 86(2) place a BOP on the accused; general exceptions are different — verify, do not assume |
| 10 | **Currentness failures** | Citing s 71; old PD numbering; s 376(1) after deletion; s 420 without s 420(2) | The currentness panel in the summary §0.4 |
| 11 | **No counter-argument** | One-sided application | State the defence's best point, then answer it |
| 12 | **No landing** | "It could be X or Y." | Decide; calibrate ("likely… because…"), but decide |

## §4 The D/I marking test

> **The core test.** A deductive answer must have the rule **before** the application. An inductive answer must have the **precedent facts** and the **boundary** before the landing. Penalise both failures equally.

### 4.1 Deductive block — checklist

- [ ] The section is quoted or closely paraphrased.
- [ ] The elements are listed as **all required** (not disjunctive, unless the statute is).
- [ ] Each element gets a fact (or an express "no fact").
- [ ] The case is cited for its **unique contribution**, with a pinpoint.
- [ ] The conclusion follows from the elements, not from a vibe.

**Failure mode:** "The elements are A, B, C. On the facts, A, B, C are satisfied." → **Below Average** on Application.

### 4.2 Inductive block — checklist

- [ ] The **principle** is stated in one sentence before the cases.
- [ ] The **precedent facts** that mark the boundary are stated (not the whole case).
- [ ] The present facts are **placed** relative to the precedent: closer to X because…; distinguishable from Y because…
- [ ] The **degree** is calibrated: floor (most serious case below the line) and ceiling (least serious case above it), where the standard is a degree.
- [ ] The conclusion is a **position on the continuum**, not a binary.

**Failure mode:** "In *CFE*, the court said consent must be specific. Here, she did not consent. Therefore rape." → **Below Average** on Application (no facts, no boundary, no counter).

### 4.3 Mode-mismatch penalties

| Mismatch | Example | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Degree treated as binary | "Dishonesty: he took the money, so he was dishonest." | Application capped at Average |
| Binary treated as degree | "Penetration: the degree of penetration was substantial." | Rule/Application capped at Average |
| Factors treated as elements | "The s 11 UCTA factors are elements" (contract analogue); in CRL, "the *Barton* propositions are elements of rape" | Rule capped at Average |
| Elements treated as factors | "Consent is a factor in rape" | Rule capped at Below Average |

## §5 The defence sequencing test

| Stage | Question | Common error |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the defence **negating an element**? (s 79 mistake; s 86(2) intoxication) | Treating it as a general exception bolted on at the end |
| 2 | Has the **prima facie offence** been made out? | Running private defence before proving the actus reus |
| 3 | Does the **general exception** apply? (ss 80, 81, 84, 87–94, 96–106A) | Skipping the statutory conditions |
| 4 | **Burden/standard**: who proves what? | Assuming the accused bears the burden for every defence |
| 5 | **Consequence**: acquittal; special finding (s 84 → s 313 CPC); reduced offence (Exception 2 to s 300); mitigation | Treating all defences as acquittals |

**The sequencing rule in one line:** *element first; defence second; consequence third.*

## §6 The case-mastery test (applied to every citation)

For each case in the answer, ask four questions:

1. **Contribution** — is the proposition the case actually contributes, or a neighbouring one?
2. **Facts** — are the material facts stated (or fairly implied) so the analogy/distinction is visible?
3. **Pinpoint** — does the paragraph exist, and does it say what the answer says?
4. **Limits** — does the answer say what the case is *not* authority for, where that matters?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 4/4 | Mastered |
| 3/4 | Usable, but the analogy is thin |
| 2/4 | Name-drop |
| 1/4 | Miscitation risk |
| 0/4 | Do not cite |

**A case cited without its material facts is a 2/4 at best.** A case cited for the wrong proposition is a 1/4 and a substantive error, not a style point.

## §7 Marking protocol

1. **Cold read.** Read the attempt once without the rubric; note the misses and the moments where you had to supply the reasoning.
2. **Build the charge map the answer should have had.** Parties × offences × elements × defences × counterfactual. Compare.
3. **Score bands per criterion** using §2. Do not average; a single Below Average on Application is a real weakness even if the Writing is strong.
4. **Second-half hunt** — for each issue:
   - threshold/standard stated?
   - facts mapped to the element?
   - material facts of the case(s) used for analogy/distinction?
   - counter-argument confronted?
   - landing calibrated?
5. **Case audit** — every case: contribution, facts, pinpoint, limits (§6).
6. **Currentness audit** — s 71; PD numbering; s 376(1); s 420(2); any amended penalty.
7. **Output:**
   - scoreboard table (criterion → band → one-line reason);
   - top 3 fixes, each quoting the attempt;
   - per fix, the drill (C1–C7, E1–E10);
   - ledger entries.
8. **Stance:** strict. "Would the real marker band this Above Average on Application?" If unsure, say so. Never invent authority; verify every case cited against the vault note before commenting.

## §8 The essay rubric (C7)

| Criterion | Weight within the essay | Below Average | Average | Above Average |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Thesis** | 20% | Describes the topic; no position | A position, but hedged | An arguable thesis that answers "to what extent" and frames the reform |
| **Arguments** | 35% | One argument, repeated | Two or three arguments, some authority | Three distinct arguments, each with statute/case and a reason |
| **Counter-thesis** | 15% | None | Brief | Fairly stated and answered |
| **Reform** | 15% | One sentence | A proposal with a reason | A proposal with a mechanism, a cost, and a counter |
| **Use of authority** | 10% | Case names only | Some pinpoints | Precise, current, integrated |
| **Writing** | 5% | — | — | — |

**The quote trap.** A quoted proposition is usually overstated. The strongest opening is: "The statement is true in part, but it mistakes X for Y; the better view is Z." Do not simply agree or disagree.

**The "at least two areas" instruction.** Pick two areas and go deep. Five shallow areas score worse than two deep ones.

## §10 Calibration warnings

- **No criminal A-scripts.** The contract corpus has marked A/A+ scripts; the criminal corpus does not. Bands are provisional. Say so.
- **The generic rubric is the standard.** Do not invent a "criminal law" marking scale. Use the 40/25/30 language and the band descriptors in [[IRAC Rubrics]].
- **The exam is 3 of 4.** A brilliant answer to one question and a blank to another is a fail. Mark time discipline as part of Organisation.
- **The professor rewards the second half.** The summaries already give the first half (rule + case). The marks are in the application: material facts, analogy/distinction, counter, landing.
