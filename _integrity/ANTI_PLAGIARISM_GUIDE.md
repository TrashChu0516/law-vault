## 1. WHAT PLAGIARISM MEANS IN LAW SCHOOL

Legal writing is different from other academic writing. In law:

| Is it plagiarism? | Example |
|-------------------|---------|
| ❌ **YES** | Copying a paragraph from McKendrick's textbook into your essay without citation |
| ❌ **YES** | Paraphrasing a judge's reasoning from *BOM v BOK* without citing the case |
| ❌ **YES** | Using someone else's case brief structure and ratio formulation as your own work |
| ❌ **YES** | Submitting an IRAC answer where the assistant wrote the application section |
| ✅ **NO** | Stating "Consideration must move from the promisee" (common knowledge — every law student knows this) |
| ✅ **NO** | Citing *Chwee Kin Keong v Digilandmall* for the proposition that an online listing is an ITT |
| ✅ **NO** | Using a standard IRAC structure (everyone uses IRAC — it's a methodology, not a source) |

## 2. THE FOUR-TIER CITATION SYSTEM

### Tier 1: Direct Quotation — ALWAYS in quotes + pinpoint

```markdown
✅ CORRECT:
As Phang JA held, "the proportionality framework represents a more nuanced approach..."

❌ WRONG:
As Phang JA held, the proportionality framework represents a more nuanced approach...
→ Missing quotation marks. This is plagiarism even if you cite the source.

✅ CORRECT SHORT QUOTE:
The Court of Appeal described the penalty as "extravagant, exorbitant, or unconscionable" (*Denka Advantech v Seraya Energy* [2020] SGCA at [45]).

✅ CORRECT LONG QUOTE (block quote):
> The question is not whether the clause is a penalty in the sense of being
> extravagant, but whether it imposes a detriment on the contract-breaker
> out of all proportion to any legitimate interest of the innocent party in
> the enforcement of the primary obligation.
>
> — *Cavendish Square Holding v Makdessi* [2015] UKSC 67, [32] (Lords Neuberger and Sumption)
```

### Tier 2: Paraphrase of Legal Principle — ALWAYS cited to authority

```markdown
✅ CORRECT:
The Singapore Court of Appeal has held that where an offeree has actual knowledge
of the offeror's unilateral mistake as to a term of the contract, the offeree
cannot enforce the contract (*Chwee Kin Keong v Digilandmall* [2005] 1 SLR(R) 502).

❌ WRONG:
The Singapore Court of Appeal has held that where an offeree has actual knowledge
of the offeror's unilateral mistake, the offeree cannot enforce the contract.
→ Missing citation. Even though this is a paraphrase, you must cite the case.
```

### Tier 3: Assertion About the Law — ALWAYS supported by authority

```markdown
✅ CORRECT:
Under Singapore law, a party may terminate a contract in four situations: (1) express
contractual right, (2) breach of a condition, (3) breach of an innominate term with
serious consequences, or (4) repudiatory breach (*RDC Concrete v Sato Kogyo*
[2007] 4 SLR(R) 413 (CA)).

❌ WRONG:
Under Singapore law, a party may terminate a contract in four situations.
→ This is a specific legal framework from a specific case. You must cite it.
```

### Tier 4: Your Own Analysis — MARKED as your argument

```markdown
✅ CORRECT:
It is submitted that the RDC Concrete framework, while comprehensive, fails to
adequately address the situation where a party terminates on multiple concurrent
grounds. The framework treats each ground in isolation, but in practice...

→ "It is submitted" / "It is argued" / "In my view" signals YOUR analysis.
   No citation needed for YOUR argument.
```

## 4. PARAPHRASING — THE 3-STEP SAFE METHOD

When you read a source and want to use its ideas in your own writing:

### Step 1: READ → Close the source.

You cannot paraphrase with the source open in front of you. Read it. Understand it. **Close the tab or book.**

### Step 2: WAIT → 2 minutes minimum.

Let the memory settle. The goal is to write from understanding, not from visual memory of the text.

### Step 3: WRITE → From memory, in your own words.

If you find yourself reaching for the same sentence structure or distinctive vocabulary as the original, you didn't wait long enough. Try again.

### The Self-Test

After paraphrasing, ask:
- Could someone identify the original source just from reading my paraphrase?
- If YES → it's too close. Rewrite.
- If NO → you've successfully transformed it. Still cite the source.

### Example

```
ORIGINAL (McKendrick, Contract Law, 14th ed, p 45):
"The traditional approach has been to analyse the formation of a contract
in terms of offer and acceptance. An agreement is said to be reached when
one party makes an offer which is accepted by the other party."

❌ TOO CLOSE (Plagiarism):
The traditional method analyses contract formation through the lens of
offer and acceptance. An agreement occurs when one party makes an offer
and the other party accepts it.

✅ SAFE PARAPHRASE:
English contract law determines whether parties have reached agreement by
identifying an offer from one party and a corresponding acceptance from the
other — the so-called "offer and acceptance" framework.¹

¹ McKendrick, Contract Law (14th ed, 2021) 45.
```

## 6. CITATION FORMAT: OSCOLA ADAPTED FOR SINGAPORE

### Cases (Singapore)

```
Chwee Kin Keong v Digilandmall.com Pte Ltd [2005] 1 SLR(R) 502 (CA)

Format: Case Name [Year] Volume Report Page (Court)
Note: SGCA = Court of Appeal, SGHC = High Court, SGHCR = High Court Registrar
```

### Cases (English)

```
Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co [1893] 1 QB 256 (CA)

Format: Case Name [Year] Volume Report Page (Court)
Note: Pre-1993 English cases may be binding in Singapore under AELA s 3
```

### Statutes (Singapore)

```
Unfair Contract Terms Act (Cap 396, 1994 Rev Ed) s 2(1)
Misrepresentation Act (Cap 390, 1994 Rev Ed)

First reference: Full name (Cap XX, YYYY Rev Ed)
Subsequent references: UCTA; MA (define abbreviation on first use)
```

### Neutral Citations (Preferred, When Available)

```
BOM v BOK [2019] 1 SLR 349 (CA)    → traditional citation
BOM v BOK [2019] SGCA 12            → neutral citation (preferred by some instructors)

Use what your professor specifies. When in doubt, use both:
BOM v BOK [2018] SGCA 83; [2019] 1 SLR 349
```

### Textbooks

```
Andrew Phang (gen ed), The Law of Contract in Singapore (2nd ed, Academy Publishing, 2022) ch 3.

Ewan McKendrick, Contract Law (14th ed, Red Globe Press, 2021) 145.
```

### Journal Articles

```
Andrew Phang, 'The Development of Singapore Contract Law' (2020) 32 SAcLJ 1.

Format: Author, 'Title' (Year) Volume Journal First Page
```

## 7. QUOTING FROM JUDGMENTS — RULES

### When to Quote Directly

Quote directly from a judgment when:
1. The **exact language** of the judge is significant (e.g., the phrasing of a legal test)
2. The passage is so well-written that paraphrasing would lose meaning
3. You're about to critically analyse the judge's reasoning

### When NOT to Quote

Paraphrase instead when:
1. You're simply stating what the court held (use your own words + citation)
2. The passage is purely factual background
3. You're summarising a long passage

### Pinpoint References

```
✅ *BOM v BOK* [2019] 1 SLR 349 (CA) at [45]–[48].
✅ *BOM v BOK* [2019] SGCA 12 at [45].
❌ *BOM v BOK* [2019] 1 SLR 349 (CA).  → Missing paragraph numbers
```
