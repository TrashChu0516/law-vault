# ✅ Pre-Submission Verification Checklist

> **Run this before you submit ANY written work. If any box is unticked, you are not done.**
> This is the gate between "draft" and "ready to submit" — the part that keeps you safe.

---

## A. Authority & Accuracy

- [ ] Every proposition of law has a **citation** (case, statute, or other authority).
- [ ] Every case cited has a **pinpoint** (`at [para]` or `at page`).
- [ ] Every case has been **noted up** — you know whether it is still good law.
- [ ] Every statute is the **in-force version** (amendments checked on Singapore Statutes Online).
- [ ] You have **read the primary source**, not just a summary/headnote/secondary mention.
- [ ] The **edition** of every textbook is correct (1st vs 2nd ed Phang).

## B. No Unacknowledged Borrowing (Plagiarism)

- [ ] Every **direct quote** is in quotation marks with a pinpoint.
- [ ] Every **paraphrase** is genuinely in your own words AND still cited.
- [ ] No sentence is a near-verbatim copy of a source with only a word or two swapped.
- [ ] Your **analysis/argument** is clearly distinguished from the sourced law (e.g. "It is submitted…").
- [ ] Nothing copied from AI output is presented as your own work without disclosure (check your module's AI policy).

## C. Structural / Exam Discipline

- [ ] The structure matches the task: **IRAC** for problem questions, **thesis → body → conclusion** for essays.
- [ ] Every party and every issue is addressed (problem questions).
- [ ] Remedies/conclusions are stated for each claim.
- [ ] The answer **answers the question asked** (not a related question you'd rather answer).

## D. The "Soul Skill" Self-Test

Before submitting, ask yourself — honestly:

1. **Can I explain the ratio of every case I cited, in my own words, without looking?**
2. **Do I know why I cited each authority — what work it is doing in my argument?**
3. **Could I defend this answer out loud in a seminar?**

If the answer to any is "no," the work is not *yours* yet — go back and rebuild the weak parts. The goal is competence, not completion.
